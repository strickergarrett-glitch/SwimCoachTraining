What Elite Butterflyers Do Differently

  • The undulation flows from the chest — not the hips
  • Two dolphin kicks per stroke cycle — the second kick drives the pull
  • Entry is fingertips-first, shoulder-width — no crossing over
  • The catch is a high-elbow EVF — same as freestyle
  • The breath is forward, not upward — chin just clears the water
  • Recovery is low and fast — skimming over the surface
  • Every underwater off the wall is the fastest part of the race
The Butterfly Principle
"Butterfly is not about strength — it is about rhythm.
Master the undulation. The power follows the wave."
🎯

Core Mechanics

1 · Body Undulation
The Engine of Butterfly

The Wave Motion

Butterfly is driven by a wave that passes through the entire body — from chest to hips to feet. It is NOT an up-and-down bobbing motion. The hips rise as the chest presses down, and vice versa.

  • Chest press: Press chest DOWN as arms enter — hips rise
  • Hip drive: Hips press down as arms pull — upper body rises for breath
  • Think "chest → hips → knees → feet" — a sequential wave
  • The undulation should feel effortless — fighting it is what makes butterfly exhausting
FeelA smooth wave passing through your body from front to back. If your lower back aches, you are bending — not undulating. The movement is sequential, not simultaneous.
2 · The Double Dolphin Kick
Timing Is Everything

Two Kicks Per Stroke Cycle

There are two dolphin kicks in every butterfly stroke cycle. The timing of these kicks is critical to an efficient, sustainable butterfly.

  • First kick: Occurs at hand entry — drives hips down and sets up the pull
  • Second kick: Occurs during the pull push phase — accelerates the body forward
  • Kick is driven from the hips — knees bend slightly, ankles whip
  • Feet stay together throughout — a single-fin motion
  • Kick amplitude: moderate — not too large (inefficient) or too small (no propulsion)
FeelThe second kick should feel like it's pushing you through the water as your hands exit. The first kick should feel like it's setting up the catch.
3 · The Pull — Keyhole Pattern
Maximum Propulsion

Wide Out, Narrow In

The butterfly pull traces a "keyhole" or "hourglass" shape — wide outsweep to set the catch, narrow powerful insweep under the body, then push to exit.

  • Entry: Shoulder-width, fingertips first, thumbs slightly down
  • Outsweep/catch: High elbow EVF — same as freestyle
  • Insweep: Hands come toward center under chest — maximum power phase
  • Finish: Hands push back past hips — exit thumbs down
  • Hands should reach shoulder-width at the catch, then narrow to hip-width under the chest
FeelSolid pressure on the palms throughout the insweep. The pull should feel like you are moving the water from in front of you to behind you in one continuous line.
4 · Entry & Recovery
Speed vs. Drag

The entry and recovery must be clean and efficient — excess drag here destroys butterfly rhythm for the rest of the stroke cycle.

  • Recovery: Low and wide — arms skim over the water surface, not high in the air
  • Entry: Shoulder-width — arms enter simultaneously, fingertips first
  • Never cross the hands on entry — this kills the catch and causes crossover
  • Recovery should be relaxed — if it feels tight, your pull exited too late
5 · Breathing
Forward Not Upward

Butterfly breathing is the most technique-dependent breath in all four strokes. Getting it wrong creates massive drag. Getting it right costs almost nothing.

  • Head moves FORWARD with the body's natural rise — not lifted independently
  • Chin just clears the water — eyes look forward, not up
  • Breath occurs at the end of the insweep as arms pass the hips
  • Head drops back into the water before arms re-enter
  • Sprint butterfly: every 2–3 strokes · Distance: every stroke or every 2
FeelThe water lapping at your chin — not clearing your entire face. A low breath is a fast breath in butterfly.

Top 10 Drills

Key: All butterfly drills should be done with fins initially. Fins teach the undulation rhythm and reduce fatigue so you can focus on technique, not survival.

01
Body Dolphin (with fins)

Arms at sides, dolphin kick only. No arms. Focus purely on the wave motion passing from chest to feet. This is the most fundamental butterfly drill.

FeelA wave starting at the chest as it presses down, rolling through the hips and out through the feet. Smooth and continuous — not jerky.
MistakeBending only at the hips — the wave must start at the CHEST, not the waist.
02
Underwater Dolphin Kick on Stomach

Push off the wall in streamline on your stomach, dolphin kick underwater for the full length if possible. Builds kick power and undulation pattern simultaneously.

FeelBody accelerating with each kick — hips driving down, feet whipping.
MistakeKicking from the knees — power must come from the core and hips.
03
Single-Arm Butterfly

One arm pulls while the other extends forward. Alternate which arm pulls. Isolates each arm's pull pattern and is easier to maintain than full butterfly.

FeelThe pull arm following the keyhole pattern — out wide, in narrow, push back.
MistakeLosing the kick timing — continue the double dolphin kick on every stroke.
04
3-Kick Butterfly (with fins)

Take 3 dolphin kicks before each arm pull. Exaggerates the undulation rhythm and forces the kick to set up the pull properly.

FeelBuilding momentum with 3 kicks, then the pull feels like it "catches" on the wave.
MistakeStopping the undulation during the extra kicks — keep the wave flowing continuously.
05
Chest-Down Entry Drill

Focus specifically on pressing the chest DOWN immediately after hand entry. This creates the hip rise that drives the first kick and sets up the catch.

FeelHands enter, chest instantly presses toward the pool bottom, hips rise, first kick follows naturally.
MistakeSkipping the chest press — without it the hips stay flat and the undulation never begins.
06
Zip-Up Butterfly

Swim butterfly but keep the elbows tucked against the body during recovery — a "zip-up" motion. Exaggerates a low, fast, efficient recovery pattern.

FeelArms barely clearing the water surface on recovery — fast and relaxed.
MistakeLifting arms too high on recovery — think "skim the surface" not "soar over it."
07
Fist Butterfly

Swim butterfly with clenched fists to load the forearm during the catch and insweep phase. Same principle as freestyle fist drill.

FeelForearm and elbow creating pressure during the insweep — not just fingertips.
MistakeLosing the keyhole shape with fists — maintain the wide-out, narrow-in pattern.
08
Breathing Drill — Every Stroke

Breathe on every single stroke, focusing on keeping the chin forward (not upward). Teaches efficient, low breathing position.

FeelChin just clearing the water as the body rises naturally — not as a separate head lift.
MistakeEyes looking upward — eyes should look forward with chin on the water surface.
09
Vertical Dolphin Kick

Tread water using only dolphin kick in the deep end — arms crossed on chest. Feet together, wave motion from the core. 30-second intervals.

FeelRising and falling with each kick beat — core initiating, ankles finishing.
MistakeSeparating the feet — they must stay locked together throughout.
10
Full Butterfly with Fins — DPS Focus

Full butterfly stroke with fins, focusing on maximum distance per stroke. Count strokes per 25 — aim for 6–9 strokes per 25m for high school swimmers.

FeelEach stroke covering maximum distance — the pull and kick both driving the glide forward.
MistakeRushing — butterfly power comes from patient timing, not fast stroking.

Race Strategy

Sprint · Rhythm First

50 Fly — 2 Lengths of Perfect Butterfly

The 50 fly is won by the swimmer who maintains rhythm longest. Most swimmers rush their stroke and lose the undulation in the last 15m. Stay patient — let the wave carry you.

Race Model

  • Start + underwater: Max dolphin kicks — 5+ off the block
  • 1st 25: Controlled power — don't sprint the undulation away
  • Turn: Two-hand touch → max underwater → breakout on perfect timing
  • Last 25: Drive the second kick — it's the power kick on every stroke
Quick Time Drops
  • Longer underwater off start → saves 0.2–0.4 sec
  • Lower breathing position → saves 0.15–0.25 sec
  • Maintain undulation to the wall → saves 0.3–0.5 sec
Sprint-Endurance

100 Fly — The Back 50 Is Where It's Won

Every high school swimmer can have a great first 50 in butterfly. Elite 100 fly swimmers have a great SECOND 50. That requires training the back half in every speed session.

Pacing Model

  • First 50: Controlled — feel powerful, not panicked
  • 50m turn: Perfect two-hand touch, max underwater
  • Third 25: Maintain undulation — do NOT let it go flat
  • Last 25: Drive the second kick on every stroke — it is your only fuel source

Split Target

Goal time × 0.46 = ideal first 50 split.
A 58.0 goal → first 50 in ~26.7.
If you go out in 25.5, the back 50 will be 34+.

Endurance Event

200 Fly — Rhythm Over Everything

The 200 fly is arguably the most mentally challenging race in swimming. It requires maintaining perfect technique for 8 lengths while lactic acid builds relentlessly. Rhythm is the only way through it.

4-Split Model

  • 1st 50: Longest strokes, easiest rhythm — you should feel too slow
  • 2nd 50: Lock in — every stroke identical, breathing every 2
  • 3rd 50: Mental toughness — second kick must stay sharp
  • 4th 50: Survival mode — maintain undulation above all else
Warning Sign

If your undulation disappears and the stroke becomes flat arm-pulling in the final 50, you went out too fast. Train the 3rd and 4th 50 specifically in your sessions.

Race-Day Checklist
  • ✓
    Underwaters: Practice 3 full underwater breakouts in warm-up to confirm your dolphin kick distance.
  • ✓
    Two-hand touch: Every warm-up turn is a mandatory two-hand practice.
  • ✓
    Rhythm cue: During the race, cue "chest down" on every entry — it triggers the whole undulation.
  • ✓
    Second kick: Remind yourself — the second kick drives the pull. Never let it die.
⚠️

Top 10 Mistakes

Mistake 1

Bending at the Hips (Not Undulating)

The most common butterfly error. The wave must start at the CHEST, not fold at the waist. Hip-only movement creates a "worm" pattern with no forward momentum.

FixBody dolphin drill daily — chest initiates every wave. Keep returning to this foundation.
Mistake 2

Only One Dolphin Kick Per Stroke

Butterfly requires TWO kicks per stroke cycle. The second kick — which occurs during the pull — is the most powerful and most commonly dropped kick in high school butterfly.

Fix3-kick butterfly drill — exaggerates kick awareness. Count "1-2, 1-2" on every stroke cycle.
Mistake 3

Lifting the Head to Breathe

Independent head lifting creates drag and breaks the undulation pattern. The head should rise with the body's natural wave — chin just clearing the water, eyes forward.

FixBreathe-every-stroke drill — force the chin-forward position on every breath.
Mistake 4

High Recovery (Windmill Arms)

Arms swinging high in the air on recovery create unnecessary drag and disrupt timing. Arms should skim low over the water surface — fast and relaxed.

FixZip-up recovery drill — arms barely clear the water on every recovery.
Mistake 5

Crossover Entry

Hands entering inside shoulder-width causes the arms to cross and kills the catch position. Entry should be at exact shoulder-width, not inside it.

FixEntry drill — place entry at shoulder width on every rep. Video from front to check.
Mistake 6

No Chest Press on Entry

Entering without pressing the chest down immediately loses the first kick and the beginning of the undulation. Chest must press DOWN as arms enter — this drives the hips up and sets up the catch.

FixChest-down entry drill — exaggerate the chest press on every rep.
Mistake 7

Weak Second Kick

The second kick — which should drive the pull — is often absent or tiny in fatigued swimmers. When the second kick dies, the stroke loses all its power and forward momentum.

FixVertical dolphin kick training builds the specific muscles for the second kick.
Mistake 8

Early Breathing (Before Arms Pass Hips)

Breathing before the arms finish the push creates drag right at the power phase. The breath should begin as arms pass the hips — not before.

FixSingle-arm drill with breathing focus — feel the breath's timing relative to arm exit.
Mistake 9

Breathing Every Stroke in Sprint Fly

Breathing every stroke in the 50 or 100 fly dramatically slows the stroke rate and kills the undulation rhythm. Sprint butterfly: every 2–3 strokes. The non-breathing strokes are always faster.

Mistake 10

Going Out Too Fast — Losing the Wave

When butterfly goes out too fast, the undulation disappears first. Once the wave is gone, butterfly becomes an exhausting arm-pull survival stroke. Keep the wave — it is the entire stroke.
