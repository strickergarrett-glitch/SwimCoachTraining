What Elite Backstrokers Do Differently

  • High hips — body nearly horizontal, ears in the water
  • Shoulder rotation 45–60° drives the pull power
  • Entry is pinky-first, arm fully extended past the shoulder line
  • High elbow bent pull — not a straight-arm sweep
  • 6-beat kick drives rotation and maintains position
  • Flags mastery — same stroke count to the wall every time
  • Backstroke turn is a race within a race — practice it daily
The Backstroke Principle
"Your rotation is your engine.
Your kick is your balance.
Your flags are your GPS."

Core Mechanics

1 · Body Position
Foundation

High Hip Position

Hips should ride at or very near the surface. The most common flaw in high school backstroke is sitting in the water — hips too low, creating drag and slowing rotation.

  • Head: Neutral — ears in the water, chin slightly tucked, eyes look up
  • Hips: Use kick to keep them at surface level
  • Core: Engaged throughout — prevents hip sag on the pulling side
  • Body line: Think "plank on water" — stiff and flat between strokes
FeelThe water surface should lap at your ear level. If you can see your chest, your hips are probably sinking.
2 · Entry & Extension
Sets Up Everything

Pinky-First Entry

The recovering arm enters pinky-first, directly behind the shoulder — NOT across the body centerline. Crossing over causes fish-tailing and kills rotation.

  • Entry at the 11 o'clock or 1 o'clock position (not 12)
  • Arm enters fully extended — reach as far back as possible
  • Immediately after entry, press down and outward to set the catch
  • Entry should be silent — splashing means you're slapping, not slicing
FeelYour arm entering the water like a blade, index finger last. A smooth silent entry means good arm angle.
3 · High Elbow Pull
Most Power

Bent-Arm Pull Pattern

Unlike the old "straight arm windmill" technique, elite backstroke uses a bent elbow at approximately 90° at the deepest point of the pull — maximizing water grip.

  • After entry: press hand outward and downward to set catch
  • Elbow bends to ~90° as hand presses toward feet
  • Hand stays outside the body line during the pull
  • Finish: push hand toward thigh, then exit thumb-first
FeelPressure on the entire palm through the pull. If pressure disappears mid-stroke, you slipped off the catch.
4 · Rotation
Power Engine

Shoulder rotation of 45–60° per side is where backstroke power comes from. The pulling shoulder drops, the recovering shoulder rises — and this drives the stroke.

  • Think: "rotate toward the pulling arm"
  • Hip rotation follows shoulder — they move together
  • Don't over-rotate: beyond 60° creates balance issues
  • Kick drives and controls rotation timing
5 · Kick
6-Beat Flutter
  • Continuous 6-beat kick — same mechanics as freestyle kick on your back
  • Drive from the hip — knee barely bends, ankle whips
  • Kick width stays narrow — toes should break the surface only slightly
  • Kick maintains body position AND drives rotation
FeelFeet fluttering just at the surface — you should see small ripples, not big splashes.
6 · Flags & Turns
Free Time

Master the Flags

The backstroke flags are 5m from the wall. Every elite backstroker knows exactly how many strokes from the flags to the wall — and hits the turn perfectly every time.

  • Count strokes from flags: typically 3–5 for high school swimmers
  • Practice this count in every single turn during workouts
  • The flip turn: rotate to stomach on the last stroke, flip, push off on your back
  • Underwater: 3–5 dolphin kicks before breakout
FeelSeeing the flags and immediately locking into your count. No looking at the wall — trust your stroke count.

Top 10 Drills

Key: Backstroke drills are best done with a snorkel or in calm water. Focus on what you feel — you cannot see yourself swimming backstroke.

01
Single-Arm Backstroke

One arm extended above head, pull with the other only. Opposite arm stays still at side. Rotate fully on each stroke.

FeelFull shoulder rotation per stroke — shoulder nearly pointing at the ceiling on each pull.
MistakeNot rotating enough — the whole point is exaggerated rotation to train the hip-shoulder connection.
02
Catch-Up Backstroke

Wait for the recovering arm to reach full extension before pulling. Slows everything down and isolates each phase.

FeelA brief pause at full extension — both arms briefly in "Superman" position above your head.
MistakeRushing into the pull — deliberately pause and feel the full extension.
03
Fist Backstroke

Swim backstroke with clenched fists. Forces the forearm and bent-elbow pull pattern to engage.

FeelForearm pressure during the pull phase — not just fingertip pushing.
MistakeStraight arming it — bend that elbow to 90° even with fists.
04
Kick on Back (Streamline)

Arms in streamline above head, kick on your back. Builds core stability and back-kick technique simultaneously.

FeelHips floating at surface, small foot ripples, not large splashes.
MistakeLetting hips sag — engage core actively throughout.
05
3-Stroke / Switch Drill

3 strokes on left, 3 strokes on right, alternating. Exaggerates each side's rotation and pull mechanics.

FeelDistinct shoulder drop on the pulling side, clean entry on the recovering side.
MistakeLosing body position between switches — stay tight and stable.
06
High Elbow Drill (Dry Land)

Standing or lying on bench: practice the bent-arm pull pattern from entry to exit. No water needed — grooves the joint pattern.

FeelElbow bending to 90° as the "hand" presses toward feet. Exit thumb-first.
MistakeKeeping the elbow straight — the bent pull is the whole point.
07
Flags Count Practice

Swim at moderate pace. Every time you pass under the flags, count your strokes to the wall. Find and memorize your count.

FeelLocked-in certainty about your distance — no need to turn head or search for the wall.
MistakeVarying effort — count at race pace, drill pace, and easy pace (they may differ).
08
Rotation on Kickboard

Kick on back holding kickboard on chest. Rotate the kickboard side to side with each kick beat — exaggerates hip rotation feel.

FeelHips pivoting with each kick, body rotating cleanly side to side.
MistakeIsolating rotation to just the board — the whole torso should rotate.
09
Entry & Extension Drill

Slow-motion backstroke focusing only on the entry — arm enters pinky-first, reaches full extension before next arm moves.

FeelSilent entry like a blade, maximum reach behind the head.
MistakeSplashing on entry — means the hand is entering flat. Rotate wrist so pinky leads.
10
Underwater Dolphin Kick on Back

Push off wall on back in streamline. 5+ dolphin kicks before breakout. Arms tight above head, core locked.

FeelBody undulating smoothly — hips drive up, feet drive down. Speed building with each kick.
MistakeKicking from knees — power must come from the core/hip, whipping to ankles.

Race Strategy

Sprint · Skill-Dominant

50 Back — Where Time Is Made & Lost

The underwater off the start and the one turn are the two biggest time factors in the 50 back. Nail both and the race is largely won.

Race Model

  • 0–15m: Underwater off the block — 5+ dolphin kicks, explode to surface
  • 15–35m: Build stroke rate, rotation driving every pull
  • 35m (flags): Lock in count — perfect turn
  • Final 25: Max kick, lengthen stroke, no wasted motion
Quick Time Drops
  • Add 1 underwater dolphin kick → saves ~0.20 sec
  • Consistent flags count = perfect turns every time
  • Reduce 2 strokes per length → saves ~0.25 sec
Sprint-Endurance

100 Back — Rotation Is Your Fuel

The 100 back is won by swimmers who maintain their rotation in the last 25. When rotation dies, the stroke flattens, drag increases, and times blow up.

Pacing Model

  • First 50: Strong but not maximal — feel controlled power
  • Third 25: Rotation must stay — don't let the stroke flatten
  • Last 25: Drive the kick, maintain rotation, count flags perfectly
Aerobic Event

200 Back — Even Split Strategy

The 200 back is the most aerobic sprint in backstroke. Consistent rotation and a locked-in stroke count per lap is the fastest way to swim it.

4-Split Model

  • 1st 50: Relaxed and long — establish rhythm
  • 2nd 50: Lock in stroke count — breathe every 2
  • 3rd 50: Build — kick picks up
  • 4th 50: Everything you have left
Race-Day Checklist
  • ✓
    Flags: Confirm your stroke count from flags to wall during warm-up.
  • ✓
    Underwaters: Practice 2–3 full underwater breakouts in warm-up.
  • ✓
    On the block: Deep breath, set feet grip, visualize your entry arc.
  • ✓
    Rotation cue: Remind yourself — "rotate toward the pull" on every stroke.

Top 10 Mistakes

Mistake 1

Hips Sitting Low

Low hips create drag just like in freestyle. The kick must keep hips at the surface. If you look like you're sitting in a chair, your hips are too low.

FixKick-on-back streamline drill — feel hips floating up to surface.
Mistake 2

Crossover Entry

Arm crossing the body centerline on entry causes S-pattern swimming and kills rotation. Entry must be at the shoulder line, NOT across it.

FixEntry drill with focus on pinky-first, shoulder-width entry. Video from overhead.
Mistake 3

Straight-Arm Pull

A straight-arm pull scoops water sideways instead of backward. The bent-elbow (90°) pull is dramatically more powerful and efficient.

FixFist drill + single-arm drill with focus on elbow bend at depth of pull.
Mistake 4

No Shoulder Rotation

A flat backstroke is a slow backstroke. Without rotation, you lose reach, pull power, and recovery clearance. Rotate 45–60° per side minimum.

Fix3-stroke switch drill + single-arm drill.
Mistake 5

Head Popping Up

Lifting the head to look for the wall creates drag and breaks body position. Trust your flags count — you should never need to look for the wall.

FixFlags count practice daily until the count is automatic.
Mistake 6

Flat Entry (Splashing)

A flat hand entry creates drag and a noisy splash. The pinky-in entry slices through the water cleanly and sets up the catch properly.

FixEntry drill — aim for zero splash on every rep.
Mistake 7

Early Pull Exit

Exiting the pull before the hand pushes past the thigh loses the most powerful phase. Full extension at exit = thumb brushes thigh.

Mistake 8

Knee-Driven Kick

Same as freestyle — large knee bend creates drag. Hip-initiated flutter kick with floppy ankles is the standard.

FixKick on back with hands on hips, feel the thigh driving each kick beat.
Mistake 9

Ignoring the Turn

A sloppy backstroke turn with weak underwaters can cost 1–2 full seconds per race. The turn is practiced skill — it doesn't improve without deliberate work.

FixTurn-only sets: 10+ turns per session, perfect execution every rep.
Mistake 10

Over-Rotating

Rotating past 60° causes the stroke to become unbalanced and the kick to lose effectiveness. 45–60° is the sweet spot — any more and you're swimming sideways.
