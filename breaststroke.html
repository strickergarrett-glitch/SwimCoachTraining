What Elite Breaststrokers Do Differently

  • The pullout off every wall is the fastest moment in the race — they maximize it
  • Knees stay inside hip-width — narrow kick = less drag
  • Head stays in line during the pull — no bobbing
  • The glide is a weapon, not a rest — they use it to accelerate
  • Hands never separate wider than the shoulders during pull
  • Hip drive powers the kick — not just the legs
  • Timing is everything: pull–breathe–kick–glide in a perfect rhythm
The Breaststroke Equation
"Breaststroke is 50% timing, 30% technique, 20% fitness.
Fix the timing first. Everything else follows."

Core Mechanics

1 · The Pullout (Underwater)
Free Speed — Most Important

Breaststroke Pullout

The pullout off every start and turn is the fastest part of the race. It consists of a streamline, one underwater pull to the thighs, one dolphin kick, and then a breakout. Mastering it alone can drop 2–3 seconds.

  • Streamline: Tight squeeze, full extension, head neutral
  • Underwater pull: One long pull all the way to thighs — do NOT start the pull too early
  • Dolphin kick: One kick as arms recover to streamline
  • Breakout: First breaststroke pull begins just as you hit swimming speed
FeelGliding forward with momentum, then the pull accelerates you further. The dolphin kick should feel like a boost, not a recovery.
2 · The Pull
Outsweep → Insweep → Recovery

Breaststroke Pull Pattern

The breaststroke pull is a heart-shaped pattern. The outsweep sets the catch, the insweep generates propulsion, and the recovery shoots hands forward.

  • Outsweep: Hands press outward to shoulder-width, elbows stay high
  • Catch: Elbows bend as hands face backward — high elbow catch
  • Insweep: Hands sweep inward and back under the chest powerfully
  • Recovery: Shoot hands forward — fast and tight to the body surface
  • Hands never pull wider than shoulder-width — wider = drag, not propulsion
FeelWater pressure building on the palms during the insweep. If you feel pressure during the outsweep, you're pulling too early.
3 · The Kick
Most Unique in Swimming

Whip Kick Mechanics

The breaststroke kick is the primary propulsive force — more so than in any other stroke. It must be symmetrical, narrow, and powerful.

  • Recovery: Heels draw toward glutes — knees stay inside hip-width
  • Catch: Feet flex and rotate outward (dorsiflex + evert)
  • Drive: Heels push outward and backward in a circular arc
  • Finish: Feet snap together — squeeze the finish for extra propulsion
  • Knees must stay inside the hip line — wide knees = massive drag
FeelThe "catch" phase — the moment your feet rotate outward and you feel water on the soles. Then drive and snap.
4 · Timing — The Master Key
Most Important Concept

Pull–Breathe–Kick–Glide

Breaststroke timing is what separates good from elite. The phases must flow in exact sequence — there should never be a pause or overlap between pull and kick.

  • Pull: Outsweep → insweep → hands meet under chest
  • Breathe: Head rises naturally with the pull — NOT an active lift
  • Kick: Begins as hands shoot forward — the kick and recovery overlap
  • Glide: Body fully streamlined, momentum carries you forward
FeelOne fluid motion — like a wave passing through your body from head to toe. No dead spots, no stops.
Common Timing FlawBreathing too late (after the pull instead of during it) or kicking before hands shoot forward. Both kill momentum.
5 · Body Position in Glide
Maximize Momentum

During the glide, your body should be in a perfect streamline — arms extended, head neutral between arms, feet together and pointed. Any drag in the glide wastes the momentum you just created.

  • Head drops between arms immediately after the breath
  • Hips should be at surface level during the glide
  • Glide length varies — sprint breaststroke glides less, distance glides more

Top 10 Drills

Key: Breaststroke drills must be done slowly at first. The timing pattern is learned by feel — rushing it trains the wrong pattern.

01
Pullout Practice

From each wall, practice only the pullout: streamline → underwater pull to thighs → one dolphin kick → breakout. Full rest between each. This is the most valuable drill in the set.

FeelSpeed accelerating through the pull, then the dolphin kick maintaining it. Breakout at maximum forward momentum.
MistakeStarting the pull too early — wait until you're at full streamline speed before pulling.
02
2-Kick 1-Pull Drill

Two breaststroke kicks for every one pull. Forces the kick to be the dominant propulsive force and teaches proper kick-pull separation.

FeelEach kick driving you forward with the body in streamline. The pull becomes secondary.
MistakeRushing the second kick — each kick should be deliberate and powerful.
03
Kick on Back

Breaststroke kick on your back, arms at sides. You can watch your knees and feet to check width and ankle position. Essential for self-correction.

FeelHeels drawing to glutes, feet rotating outward to catch, then snapping together at finish.
MistakeKnees spreading wider than hips — visible from above and dramatically slows you down.
04
Breaststroke Kick with Board

Kickboard held in front, arms extended. Isolates the kick completely. Breathe by lifting eyes forward, not lifting the whole head.

FeelForward momentum through the kick finish — feet snapping together should surge you forward.
MistakeSlow, wide, lazy kick. Each kick should feel like an explosion, not a paddle.
05
Arms-Only Pull (Pull Buoy)

Pull buoy between legs, swim breaststroke arms only. Isolates the pull pattern and timing. Focus on the outsweep→insweep→shoot sequence.

FeelPressure building only during the insweep. The hand shoot forward should be forceful and tight to the body.
MistakePulling hands too wide — they should never exceed shoulder-width during the pull.
06
3-Glide Drill

Take one full breaststroke pull and kick, then hold the streamline glide position for a count of 3 before the next stroke. Exaggerates the glide and builds streamline body position.

FeelFull extension, head neutral, feet together and pointed during the 3-count. Maximum streamline.
MistakeStarting the pull too soon — force yourself to hold the count even as you slow down.
07
Timing Drill — Hands Lead Kick

Exaggerate the timing: shoot hands forward fully before the kick begins. This corrects the most common timing error (kick starting too early).

FeelA brief overlap where your hands are fully extended before your feet start the drive.
MistakeReverting to simultaneous hands-and-kick — slow down and be deliberate about the sequence.
08
Breaststroke Fist Drill

Swim breaststroke with clenched fists. Forces the forearm to load up during the insweep and teaches the high-elbow catch position.

FeelForearm pressure during the insweep rather than just palm pressure.
MistakeWidening the pull to compensate for fists — keep the same shoulder-width pattern.
09
Head-Lead Kick Drill

Float face down, arms at sides, kick only. Head stays in the water throughout (breathe by turning to the side). Forces you to feel the kick propulsion without arm help.

FeelEach kick cycle moving you forward — if you're sinking, the kick is too slow or too wide.
MistakeLifting the head to breathe — turn to the side to maintain body position.
10
Underwater Breaststroke Pull

Swim one full length of underwater breaststroke — one pullout plus as many underwater pull-kick cycles as possible before surfacing. Builds lung capacity and strengthens the underwater pull pattern.

FeelStreamlining fully between each underwater pull-kick cycle. Maximum efficiency underwater.
MistakeSurfacing too early — challenge yourself to stay under as long as possible (safely).

Race Strategy

Sprint · Timing Critical

50 Breast — Pullout Is Everything

In the 50, a perfect pullout off the start can be 30–40% of your total race. Sprint breaststroke uses a shorter glide and faster tempo — think explosive, not rhythmic.

Race Model

  • Start pullout: Max distance — aim for 10m+ before first pull
  • First 15m: Explosive tempo — short glide, aggressive kick
  • 15–35m: Maintain tempo, perfect timing on every stroke
  • Final 15m: Turn pullout → max effort → touch wall on a kick finish
Quick Time Drops
  • Longer pullout → saves 0.3–0.5 sec
  • Narrower kick (practice daily) → saves 0.2–0.4 sec
  • Faster hand recovery (shoot hands) → saves 0.1–0.2 sec
Sprint-Endurance

100 Breast — Stroke Integrity Wins

The 100 breast is lost in the third 25 when kick width widens under fatigue. The swimmer who maintains narrow knees and snappy kick finish in the third 25 almost always wins.

Pacing Model

  • First 50: Strong, slightly below max — feel controlled
  • Third 25: Fight for the kick — knees in, snap the finish
  • Last 25: Touch on a KICK, not a pull — time your finish

The Touch

Two hands simultaneously on the touchpad — must happen at the end of a full arm extension, not mid-stroke. Finish timing: arrive at the wall mid-glide with arms extended.

Aerobic Power

200 Breast — Rhythm Is Your Weapon

The 200 breast is a race of rhythm. Find your tempo on lap 1 and hold it exactly through lap 8. Athletes who change tempo in the middle almost always fade at the end.

4-Split Model

  • 1st 50: Establish rhythm — don't go out too fast
  • 2nd 50: Lock in — every stroke identical
  • 3rd 50: Mental toughness — maintain kick integrity
  • 4th 50: Every ounce — finish with a kick on the wall
Race-Day Checklist
  • ✓
    Pullout: Practice 3 full pullouts in warm-up — confirm your breakout distance.
  • ✓
    Timing: Repeat your mantra — "pull–breathe–kick–glide" during warm-up laps.
  • ✓
    Touch: Two hands, simultaneous, on every turn and finish — no exceptions.
  • ✓
    Finish: Time your last stroke to arrive at the wall on an arm extension, not a kick.

Top 10 Mistakes

Mistake 1

Wide Knees on Kick

Knees spreading wider than hips is one of the biggest drag sources in all of swimming. It adds frontal resistance and reduces kick propulsion simultaneously.

FixKick on back, watch your knees. Video from above. Knees must stay inside the hip line on every rep.
Mistake 2

Lifting the Head to Breathe

Actively lifting the head breaks body position and delays the kick. The head should rise naturally as the pull brings the shoulders up — not as an independent movement.

FixArms-only drill with pull buoy — practice breath rising with the pull.
Mistake 3

Kick Before Hands Shoot

Starting the kick before the hands are fully extended kills the glide momentum and shortens the propulsive phase. Hands must be fully extended before the kick drives.

FixTiming drill — deliberately exaggerate the hands-lead-kick sequence.
Mistake 4

Too-Wide Pull

Hands pulling wider than the shoulders move water sideways rather than backward. The power phase of the pull is the insweep — which can only happen if the outsweep stays within shoulder-width.

FixArms-only drill — mark shoulder-width with awareness on every rep.
Mistake 5

Short or Weak Pullout

A weak pullout off the wall is free time left on the table. Pulling too early wastes the glide speed. This single skill can account for 2–3 seconds of improvement.

FixPullout-only practice sets at the start of every session — minimum 6 reps.
Mistake 6

No Ankle Catch

Failure to dorsiflex and evert the feet before the kick drive means you're pushing with the wrong surface. The soles and inside edges of the feet should face backward during the drive.

FixKick on back, watch foot position at the catch moment.
Mistake 7

Kick Finish Not Snapping

A lazy kick finish that doesn't squeeze feet together loses the final propulsive impulse. The feet should snap together like a whip at the end of the drive.

FixKickboard set: focus only on the snap-together finish of each kick.
Mistake 8

Hips Sinking in the Glide

If your hips drop during the glide, your drag increases dramatically. The streamline position must be maintained with hips up and body flat throughout the glide phase.

Fix3-glide drill — hold the position for 3 full seconds and feel if hips sag.
Mistake 9

Forgetting the Two-Hand Touch

A one-hand touch in breaststroke is an automatic disqualification. It happens most often on the last length when swimmers are fatigued. Practice two-hand touches on every single turn.

FixTurn practice sets — two-hand touch is non-negotiable on every rep.
Mistake 10

Going Out Too Fast

Breaststroke is the most affected stroke by pacing errors. Going out even one stroke-cycle too fast on the first 50 causes technique to collapse — knees widen, timing breaks — in the third 25.
